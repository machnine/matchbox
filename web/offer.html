What this means
Antibody load
How much of this patient's antibody is aimed at this particular donor, added up across every antibody that hits. More load generally means more treatment is needed to transplant safely.
What it is compared against
Every donor this patient could be offered.
Why counts, not a rank
Load depends on which antibodies a donor triggers, so many donors score identically. An exact rank would be false precision.
What this does not tell you
Nothing about organ quality, waiting time, or whether to accept.

These figures exclude every compatible donor, so they answer a narrower question than the headline: among donors this patient already reacts to, where does this offer sit.

There is no single agreed measure of how difficult an incompatible transplant will be, so all four are shown. A low total with a high single value means one dominant antibody rather than several moderate ones.

Total load
Every antibody hitting this donor, added up. cumulative
Strongest single antibody
The highest one on its own. max
Average per antibody
Total divided by how many hit. mean
Typical antibody
The middle value of those that hit. median

Antibody load and tissue matching are different things, and an offer can be good at one and poor at the other. Judging either alone hides that trade-off, so both are counted together here.

Better on both counts the donors that would beat this offer on antibody load and on tissue match at the same time. It is the number that answers “is this as good as it realistically gets for this patient”, which neither measure answers alone. A small number means waiting is unlikely to produce something better.

Tissue-match level
The UK (NHSBT) kidney matching scheme grades a donor from 1 (best) to 4 (poorest) on how many HLA-B and HLA-DR broad antigens differ between donor and recipient. Levels 1 and 2 are classed as favourable. This is a UK-specific scheme; other countries grade differently, so the level is not portable outside it.
Level 1
0 DR and 0–1 B mismatches.
Level 2
1 DR and 0 B mismatches.
Level 3
0 DR with 2 B, or 1 DR with 1 B.
Level 4
1 DR with 2 B, or 2 DR mismatches.
Load quarters
The reference donors split into four equal-sized groups by antibody load, lowest first. Heavy ties mean the groups are rarely the same size.

Only donors this patient reacts to are counted here. Compatible donors need no antibody treatment at all and are better than any of them.

Measured from real past donors. Nothing is predicted or modelled. Not a waiting-time estimate, not a judgement of organ quality, and not a recommendation to accept or decline.
